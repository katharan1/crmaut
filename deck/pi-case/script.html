1 / 14 · Performance & Intelligence

I'll walk you through how I would run P&I from 1 December: what we start with, why, how we measure success, and how we make it stick. Ten minutes, then questions.

2 / 14 · P&I should make the numbers trustworthy before scaling AI

Here is the answer up front. Five priorities, grouped into three moves: clarity, learn, act. The thread through all of them: we cannot grow a North Star 4.5 times if we do not trust what it measures. Brain comes right after, on top of trusted data. Personas are already nearly finished; we use Go Big to validate them.

3 / 14 · The North Star must grow 4.5x on a base that is partly noise

The North Star has to grow 4.5x while the client base only doubles. So growth cannot come from enrolment alone; existing clients must level up faster and drop less.

The most important signal is the drops. Because points depend on balances, every 500k CZK or a closed term deposit moves points. Clients near a threshold bounce up and down without changing behaviour, and their savings rate bounces with them. So part of our North Star is balance noise.

The pilot shows execution matters: targeted communication almost doubled enrolment. And Finance does not yet see the value, which makes proof a survival issue for the programme.

4 / 14 · Five filters narrow 30 areas to 5 priorities

Criteria, in knock-out order. One: does P&I own the outcome? The savings calculator, reward selection and native entry are squad Klient's; new scoring rules are the council's decision; CRM implementation is the CRM team's. P&I feeds them evidence.

Two: cost of delay. What must exist before Go Big or 30 June? Fénix has a floating deadline, agentic CRM and the self-learning Brain need foundations first.

Three: merge overlaps. Segment definitions and new money vs transfers are part of one metric truth; the dashboard is its output; young clients are one segment.

Four: impact on North Star and CM3, times feasibility with five analysts and our dependency on other teams.

5 / 14 · The top 5 score highest on impact and feasibility

These are the nine workstreams after merging, scored on five criteria. The top five are all three-heavy on time-criticality and feasibility.

Brain v1 has the highest potential North Star impact, but nothing is in development and George releases depend on Vienna, so it scores low on feasibility before Go Big. It starts in February in FIT+, on the segments we build now.

The Finance report needs the one metric truth first, so it follows in Q1. The collaboration model is how we work, which I cover in the governance part.

6 / 14 · The top 5 build the P&I loop from the bottom up

This is how the three pillars fit together: a loop. Measure, find, decide, act, prove, and back.

The top 5 deliberately sit at the start: you cannot close the loop without trusted measurement. Act belongs to the squads; P&I recommends, they decide.

Prove is not a separate project: every segment and every at-risk list ships with a holdout group. That is the experimentation framework missing from the list, built in from day one. The same loop can later be copied to other domains.

7 / 14 · The top 5 land before Go Big; Brain follows

Everything in the top 5 lands before Go Big on 1 February. Pilot learnings in December feed the Go Big segments in January.

After Go Big, the Finance report runs monthly on the new definitions, and Brain v1 starts in FIT+ with rules from the Go Big segments, then moves to George once Vienna capacity is secured. Personas are nearly finished: in January we pre-test the Go Big messages on them, and in February and March we compare their predictions with the real holdout results. Only if they predict well do we use them to pre-test every campaign.

The critical path is P1: it rests on one reporting lead and one data engineer. Protecting their time is my first ask.

8 / 14 · Six people cover the top 5 as personas wrap up

This is the real team: four AI engineers from the synthetic personas project, one reporting lead from financial health and one data engineer from Level Up.

The persona tool is nearly finished, so the AI engineers move onto the modelling work in the top 5: volatility simulation, pilot uplift, segments and the at-risk model. P4 and P5 are the first building blocks of Brain, so they are building Brain from day one.

One engineer finishes personas and uses them on Go Big. The thinnest skill is reporting: one person. That is a single point of failure, so the AI engineers learn the reporting stack in Q1.

9 / 14 · The list lacks a way to prove what P&I changes

Four things I would add. First and most important: a way to prove impact. Holdout groups for every campaign and list. Without that, we will never convince Finance.

Second, a driver tree that shows whether 125k is reachable and which lever gets us there.

Third, the list has data quality and new rules, but not the question in between: how much of the drops is pure balance movement. That is the evidence the council needs.

Fourth, AI compliance. Banker notes in particular are personal data.

10 / 14 · Five unknowns close within three weeks

What I do not know yet, and how I close it. The biggest is how much of the drops is balance movement: that is a data decomposition my team can do in two weeks.

Then partner capacity: George in Vienna, FIT+, CRM, data engineering. I need their planning cycles, because our dependencies sit there.

Finance's CM3 method, the pilot design to check for selection bias, and what squads and branches actually need. By week 3 I re-run the scoring with real data.

11 / 14 · P&I is judged by what others do with its output

P&I recommends and squads decide, so we are judged on what others do with our work, not on how many reports we ship.

Measure: one number for the North Star used by everyone, and less noise in level drops. Find: share of our outputs that lead to a decision. Prove: incremental level-ups and prevented drops, measured against holdouts. Trust: how satisfied the squads are with us.

Targets are proposals; I would set them after the December baseline.

12 / 14 · Shared rules for capacity keep the priorities from eroding

How the priorities survive many stakeholders. One forum with the domain lead decides the order; the P&I backlog is public, so a new request visibly displaces something else.

Capacity is explicit: 60% on priorities, 25% on requests, 15% buffer. Two AI engineers are named contacts: the one on segments for squad Klient, the one on the at-risk list for squad Bankéř. They take requests and work on priorities for that squad.

Every permanent report needs an owner and the decision it serves. Our needs from George, FIT+ and CRM go into their planning early. All squads use the same metric tree in their OKRs. And early wins buy credibility to say no later.

If asked about personas: the tool is nearly finished. We build personas for the Go Big segments, pre-test the messages in January, then compare what they predicted with what actually happened in the holdout groups.

Only if the predictions are close do we use personas to pre-test every campaign. Either way, they complement real client interviews, never replace them.
