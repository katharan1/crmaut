1 / 16 · Performance & Intelligence

I'll walk you through how I would run P&I from 1 December: what we start with, why, how we measure success, and how we make it stick. Ten minutes, then questions.

2 / 16 · P&I serves three users, and one is AI

Before priorities, who are we building for? Three users.

First, people who decide: the squads, domain leadership, Finance and the concept council. They need one truth, opportunities and proof of value. We serve them with reporting, insights and regular opportunity finding.

Second, people who act: clients in George and bankers in FIT+ and at branches. They need the right next step and the right message. That is Brain, the segments, the at-risk list, and personas, which test the message before it reaches real clients.

Third, and new: AI itself. Brain, agentic CRM and AI assistants consume our data too. So everything we build must be readable by machines: metric definitions available through an API, every recommendation and its outcome logged, and an evaluation harness so we can test quality and compliance and switch models without rebuilding. These are design principles for how we build the top 5, not an extra workstream.

3 / 16 · North Star must grow 4.5x, clients only 2.1x

The key numbers from the brief. The North Star must grow 4.5 times, from 28 thousand to 125 thousand net level-ups, while the client base only doubles. So the share of clients who level up must more than double, from about 5 to 10 percent.

22.6 thousand clients dropped a level since October, largely because points follow account balances, and the savings rate moves with the level.

And Finance says the programme is not yet profitable. For share of wallet and value-for-money NPS we have targets but no baseline, which is why one source of truth comes first.

4 / 16 · Rollout gets half of the pilot's results

Same programme, two very different results. With targeted communication in the Hradec Králové pilot, 24.6 percent of clients enrolled; with the silent start only 13. In the pilot 63 percent chose a reward; in the rollout today 34.

On both measures the rollout gets about half of what the pilot did. That is not a product problem, it is execution: communication, timing, how the reward is presented.

So the cheapest lever for Go Big is to understand exactly what made the pilot work and copy it. That is priority 3. One caveat I will check: whether pilot clients were selected differently.

5 / 16 · Five criteria decide what P&I does first

How I decided. First one gate: does P&I own the outcome? If another squad or the concept council owns it, like the savings calculator or new scoring rules, P&I supplies evidence but does not lead.

Then every area is scored one to three on five criteria. North Star impact: does it add net level-ups or stop drops? Proof of value: does it help us show SoW and CM3 to Finance? Cost of delay: does waiting past Go Big cost us? Unlocks others: does other work depend on it? And feasibility: can our team and our partner teams deliver it now?

Each criterion has a source: the level and drop data, Finance's CM3 method, the Go Big plan, interviews with the squads, and partner capacity plans. I validate the scores with the squads in week three.

And this is not a one-off: the same criteria rank every new request that comes to P&I.

6 / 16 · Four building blocks, with Brain built on top

My five areas from the list: 1, 18, 10, 3 and 7. Four building blocks, and Brain built on top of them.

Area 1, one metric truth, because today we have targets for SoW and NPS but no baseline, and part of the 22.6 thousand drops is balance noise. It delivers one clean North Star, and the evidence the concept council needs to fix the rules. It needs Finance and all squads to agree.

Area 18, pilot lessons, because the rollout gets half of what the pilot did. Ready in December, so it shapes Go Big, and it is the first evidence of what actually moves clients.

Area 10, segments and triggers for Go Big, through Digital Growth and Engagement's campaigns. These become Brain's first rules.

Area 3, a weekly at-risk list for bankers: Brain's first next-best-action use case.

And area 7, Brain v1 itself, because it has the longest lead time: George goes through Vienna. We design it and the decision log in December, and it goes live in FIT+ in March, built from the four blocks. Every segment and list carries a holdout group, so we prove impact from day one.

7 / 16 · Blocks land before Go Big, Brain in March

The four building blocks land before Go Big on 1 February. Pilot lessons in December feed the Go Big segments in January.

Brain v1 starts in December with design and the decision log, goes live in FIT+ in March on the rules from the segments and the at-risk model, then moves to George once Vienna capacity is secured. The rules evidence from P1 goes to the concept council in January. After Go Big the Finance report runs monthly. Personas pre-test the Go Big messages in January and are validated against the holdouts in February and March.

The critical path is P1: it rests on one reporting lead and one data engineer. Protecting their time is my first ask.

8 / 16 · Six people cover the top 5 as personas wrap up

This is the real team: four AI engineers from the synthetic personas project, one reporting lead from financial health and one data engineer from Level Up.

The persona tool is nearly finished, so the AI engineers move onto the modelling work in the top 5: the noise analysis, pilot uplift, segments, the at-risk model and Brain itself. They are building Brain from day one.

One engineer finishes personas and uses them on Go Big. The thinnest skill is reporting: one person. That is a single point of failure, so the AI engineers learn the reporting stack in Q1.

9 / 16 · The list lacks a way to prove what P&I changes

Four things I would add to the list. First and most important: a way to prove impact. Holdout groups for every campaign and list; without them we will never convince Finance that SoW and CM3 moved because of us.

Second, a North Star driver tree: enrolment, level-up rate and drops. It tells us whether 125 thousand is reachable and which lever gets us there.

Third, the AI foundations from the users slide: metrics available through an API, every recommendation and its outcome logged, and testing, including GDPR and the AI Act, before Brain scales.

Fourth, monitoring in the first weeks of Go Big, when volume doubles. None of these needs a separate team; they are built into the top 5.

What I do not know yet, and how I close it. The biggest is how much of the drops is balance movement: that is a data decomposition my team can do in two weeks.

Then partner capacity: George in Vienna, FIT+, CRM, data engineering. I need their planning cycles, because our dependencies sit there.

Finance's CM3 method, the pilot design to check for selection bias, and what squads and branches actually need. By week 3 I re-run the scoring with real data.

11 / 16 · P&I is judged on nine hard, automated metrics

How you can judge P&I objectively: nine metrics on one dashboard, refreshed daily. Each comes from a log, a holdout group or a system, never from a survey.

Impact: extra net level-ups from our actions, measured against holdout groups. At least 10 thousand by June, about a tenth of the gap to 125 thousand.

The NBA engine, Brain: uplift, meaning the level-up rate within 90 days of clients who got a recommendation versus a holdout, target plus 3 percentage points. And coverage: at least 90 percent of active clients have a valid next step in George or FIT+.

Synthetic personas: prediction error, how far persona predictions are from the real campaign response, at most 20 percent. And at least 80 percent of campaigns pre-tested before launch.

Data trust: at most 0.1 percent of level changes found wrong in daily reconciliation, and data fresh by the next day.

Adoption: at least 60 percent of at-risk clients contacted by a banker within a week, and at least half of the opportunities we hand to squads live within a quarter.

Targets are proposals; I set them against the December baseline. We fail if Brain shows no uplift over its holdout, or if we cannot show what we caused.

12 / 16 · Explicit rules keep the priorities alive

How I make sure the priorities happen and hold. Five mechanisms, one for each part of the problem.

Many stakeholders: clear decision rights. The domain lead sets the order in a monthly forum, the concept council owns the logic of levels and points, and P&I recommends. So conflicts have a place to be decided, not argued in corridors.

Different requests: one intake, and every request is scored on the same five criteria as the top 5, in a weekly triage. The backlog is public, so everyone sees why something waits.

Limited capacity: 60 percent of our time on the priorities, 25 on requests, 15 buffer. What we need from George, FIT+ and CRM goes into their quarterly planning early, as agreed commitments.

Getting it used: each squad has a named contact in P&I, and we build outputs together with the squads, so they own them. Early wins by January earn the credibility to say no later.

Keeping it going: I track how much capacity really goes to the top 5 and how many outputs are acted on, and we re-score the priorities every quarter. A new request is always welcome, but it openly displaces something else.

13 / 16 · The top 5 is the first turn of a learning loop

Where this is heading. P&I is not a reporting team; it is a learning loop. Measure, find, decide, act, prove, and back to measure.

The top 5 builds the first turn: P1 measures, P2 to P4 find, and P5, Brain, decides from March. The squads, bankers and campaigns act; P&I recommends, they decide. Holdouts prove what worked and feed back into the next turn.

By 30 June the first full turn is complete. After that, Brain learns from the results of its own recommendations, and because the loop is built on one truth that people and AI can read, it can be copied to other domains of the bank.

15 / 16 · Personas earn a wider role only if they predict Go Big

If asked about personas: the tool is nearly finished. We build personas for the Go Big segments, pre-test the messages in January, then compare what they predicted with what actually happened in the holdout groups.

Only if the predictions are close do we use personas to pre-test every campaign. Either way, they complement real client interviews, never replace them.
